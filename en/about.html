About voyiro

Built in Thailand, for local travel across ASEAN

We believe ride-hailing technology should make local public drivers stronger, not make them disappear. We start in Thailand and expand to neighbouring countries.

Our story

The same question, in every tourist city

Why do travellers still have to haggle over the fare when licensed public drivers are waiting all over the city? And why is the income of local public drivers falling when the number of passengers has not changed?

The same question comes up in Thailand and in neighbouring countries alike. voyiro was created to connect the three parties who already share a city — transport cooperatives, local tourism businesses and passengers — so that all of them benefit, openly, under one standard across the whole network.

We chose to work through the cooperatives rather than compete with them. The cooperative approves and oversees its members; voyiro is the system that brings those members more work.

Mission

Make local travel easy, safe and fair for everyone involved

Vision

To be the local public ride network that works everywhere in Thailand and across ASEAN

Our promise

A clear price before you board, verified drivers, and income that reaches people in the city

What we stand for

The things we will not compromise on

Legal before growth

We accept only public drivers with a licence and a correctly registered vehicle, even if that makes us grow more slowly.

We work through cooperatives

The cooperative approves and oversees its members. We do not take their work away — we bring members more of it.

Transparent down to the baht

The price is clear before you book, and every party can see exactly where the money from each trip goes, with evidence.

Money circulates in the community

Income goes back to drivers, cooperatives, hotels, tour companies and local shops.

Expansion across Thailand and ASEAN

Growing one area at a time, the right way

Everything below is a plan and a set of targets, not actual results.

Phase 1 · Chiang Mai pilot (2026)

Set up the system for songthaew and samlor cooperatives, connect LINE and the driver app, and run a three-month live trial with hotels. System demo on 21 September 2026 · Pilot target: 50 vehicles on 10 routes.

Phase 2 · Expansion to tourist cities in Thailand (2027–2028)

Phuket, Krabi, Pattaya, Hat Yai, Chiang Rai, Ayutthaya and Sukhothai, adding vehicle types to suit each city's context.

Phase 3 · Across ASEAN (2029 onwards)

Starting with Laos, Cambodia and Vietnam, working with local cooperatives under one standard that works in every country.

Three-year target: service in more than 50 cities.
